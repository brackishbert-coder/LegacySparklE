Below is a **concrete ablation plan** tailored to your ARC stack and the 13 roadmap items. The goal is to convert “sounds plausible” into **measurable deltas**, and to isolate **which mechanisms actually contribute** versus which just add complexity.

I’m going to define: **targets**, **datasets/streams**, **metrics**, **protocol**, and then a **minimal experiment matrix** you can execute without exploding combinatorics.

---

# 0) Setup: What Ablation Must Prove

For each candidate mechanism (M), you want:

1. **Effect size:** does enabling (M) move key outcome metrics in the desired direction?
2. **Specificity:** are improvements still present when controlling for confounders (amplitude, sigma, LR, etc.)?
3. **Robustness:** does it hold across seeds and across at least two distinct input regimes?
4. **Cost:** what latency/CPU/memory or instability does it introduce?

Ablation isn’t just “on vs off.” It’s **on vs matched-control**.

---

# 1) Define the Core Outcomes (Dependent Variables)

Pick a small set you will *always* report:

## A. Representation quality (manifold + mapping)

* **Quantization Error (QE):** mean distance(input, BMU weight)
* **Topographic Error (TE):** % where 1st and 2nd BMUs are non-neighbors (or your equivalent)
* **BMU diversity:** unique BMUs / window (and entropy of hits)

## B. Temporal modeling

* **TMR accuracy proxy:** distribution of prediction errors, and/or AUC for “surprise detection” on known perturbations
* **Response latency:** ms until TWO1/TMR flags after injected paradox (stress suite)

## C. Stability / health

* **Curvature summary:** mean, max, and hotspot persistence duration
* **Churn rate:** BMU jumps per window, or your churn metric
* **Freeze behavior:** frequency, duration, and false-freeze rate

## D. Fidelity (if audio loopback in scope)

* **Input-output spectral divergence:** e.g., log-magnitude spectrum distance
* **Alarm rate:** true positives vs false positives under controlled distortion

**Rule:** Every ablation run outputs a single JSON/CSV row per window + a final summary.

---

# 2) Define the Input Regimes (Independent Contexts)

You need at least **3 regimes** so results generalize:

1. **Stationary regime:** same distribution for long durations
   *Purpose:* test convergence and stability (QE/TE/curvature)
2. **Structured periodic regime:** rhythmic or repeating cycles
   *Purpose:* test multi-step TMR and coherence regulation
3. **Non-stationary regime:** gradual drift + sudden shocks
   *Purpose:* test replay, diffing, freeze, immune behavior

If you don’t want external datasets, synthesize:

* periodic: repeating “meter” sequences of BMU-driving vectors
* shocks: step-change in mean + occasional paradox injection

# 3) Baseline Configuration (“B0”)

Freeze a baseline system that is “reasonable but not fancy.”
This matters because all effects are relative to it.

**B0 recommended:**

* Fixed Adult grid (8×8)
* Current queue eviction (random)
* Current validator (static controls)
* Single-step TMR
* No heartbeat-gating
* No sigma feedback
* No multi-band babies
* No immune reset
* No distributed telemetry (unless required for performance)

You can later define B1/B2 baselines, but start with one.

---

# 4) Experimental Design: Fractional Factorial, Not Full Factorial

You have 13 candidates; full factorial is impossible.

Instead: do **targeted “one-at-a-time” (OAT)** first, then **interaction tests** only where OAT shows meaningful effect.

## Step 1: OAT screening

Run:

* B0
* B0 + M_i for each mechanism you care about

But do it with:

* **≥5 random seeds**
* each across all **3 regimes**
* report mean ± CI

This identifies which mechanisms are “worth interaction testing.”

## Step 2: Interaction testing (only for winners)

For top 3–4 mechanisms from screening, test:

* pairs: (M_a), (M_b), and (M_a+M_b)
  This catches “works alone but breaks in combination.”

---

# 5) Matched-Control Designs (Critical)

Some features change dynamics. You need controls to avoid attributing improvements to trivial side-effects.

Examples:

### (1) Entropy-weighted replay

**Control:** “priority replay” but with random priorities (same eviction churn)
This isolates “entropy” vs “just not random.”

### (5) Heartbeat-gated learning

**Control:** same average learning rate over time (duty-cycle compensated)
So gating isn’t just “lower effective LR.”

### (6) Coherence-based sigma regulation

**Control:** sigma schedule that varies with same amplitude but independent of coherence (e.g., shuffled coherence signal)
Isolates “feedback” vs “sigma variation.”

### (8) Multi-band BabySOMs

**Control:** same total Baby capacity but not split by band (e.g., 3 babies each seeing full band, or one bigger baby)
Isolates “band specialization” vs “more parameters.”

### (11) Multi-step TMR

**Control:** same-step count but using naive persistence predictor (e.g., assume next=last)
Isolates “real model” vs “just longer horizon.”

### (13) Immune reset

**Control:** “local smoothing only” without reinit
Isolates “reset” vs “regularization.”

# 6) Success Criteria (Pre-register)

Before running, define thresholds so you can say “works” without post-hoc rationalization.

Example criteria (tune to your scale):

A mechanism “passes screening” if:

* QE improves by ≥ X% **or**
* TE improves by ≥ Y% **or**
* Response latency decreases by ≥ Z ms
  AND
* does not increase max-curvature hotspot persistence by > P%
  AND
* does not increase freeze false positives by > Q%

You can set X/Y/Z/P/Q after one pilot run.

Let’s define:

* Seeds: **5**

### Phase B: Matched controls

For any mechanism that “wins,” add its matched control config.

### Phase C: Pairwise interactions (top 3)

If winners are {1,6,8}, run:

# 10) Analysis: What Plots/Tables to Produce

Minimum outputs per mechanism:

1. **Effect size table**: Δ vs baseline with CI
2. **Stability dashboard**: max curvature and freeze rate vs baseline
3. **Regime-split results**: do not average across regimes; report per regime

Decision rule:

* If it helps only in one regime and harms in another, it’s not production-safe unless gated.

---

# 11) Practical Recommendations to Avoid Self-Deception

* **Fix compute budget per run.** If a mechanism increases CPU, it may look “better” because it got more updates/time.
* **Normalize learning exposure.** Equalize number of updates, not wall-clock, if performance varies.
* **Separate training vs evaluation.** Evaluate on held-out segments or “probe injections” that aren’t learned from.
* **Randomize regime order** to avoid warm-start effects biasing one config.
